# Energy Company Upstream Organizational Benchmarking

## When to Use
- Arif asks how other energy companies structure their upstream
- Need to understand NOC vs IOC org design differences
- Analysing PETRONAS upstream in context of industry peers
- Preparing for organisational restructuring analysis (EOM, operating model changes)
- Evaluating how upstream decision-making authority is distributed

## Sources

### Primary (Annual Reports)
- Company annual reports — governance sections show official org chart
- Investor presentations — sometimes show simplified operating model
- SEC 10-K filings for IOCs (ExxonMobil, Chevron, Shell, BP, TotalEnergies)
- NOC annual reports: Saudi Aramco, PETRONAS, Petrobras, Equinor

### Secondary (Org Chart Databases)
- **OrgBrief** (orgbrief.com) — org charts with C-suite and division breakdown
- **Organimi** (organimi.com) — interactive org charts, CSV downloads
- **Creately** (creately.com) — Fortune 500 org charts
- **execgraphenergy.com** — energy-sector-specific leadership tracking
- **The Official Board** (theofficialboard.com) — executive listings

### Tertiary (Industry Analysis)
- ModalPoint — oil & gas org structure, procurement gatekeeping, commercialization
- SPE / AAPG — discipline role definitions, technical authority frameworks
- Petropt (tools.petropt.com) — upstream engineering role maps
- Industry news (Hydrocarbon Processing, Upstream Online, World Oil)

## Key Dimensions to Compare

### 1. Ownership Model
| Type | Examples | Primary Driver |
|---|---|---|
| IOC (public) | ExxonMobil, Shell, BP, Chevron, TotalEnergies | ROIC, shareholder return |
| NOC (state-owned) | Saudi Aramco, NIOC, Sonatrach, NNPC | National energy security, state revenue |
| Hybrid (entrepreneurial NOC) | PETRONAS, Equinor, Petrobras | Commercial operations + national mandate |

### 2. Upstream Org Design Pattern

| Pattern | Description | Used By |
|---|---|---|
| **Functional hierarchy** | Departments by discipline (Exploration, Drilling, Production, etc.) | Traditional NOCs (Aramco historically, NIOC, Sonatrach) |
| **Asset/Basin-based** | Semi-autonomous units by basin/asset with own P&L | Modern IOCs (ExxonMobil, Shell), entrepreneurial NOCs |
| **Matrix** | Dual reporting lines (functional + asset) | Large IOCs with complex project portfolios |
| **Hybrid** | Asset teams with central technical services pool | Most common in 2026 — best of both |

### 3. Core Upstream Disciplines
- **Subsurface**: Geology, Geophysics, Petrophysics, Reservoir Engineering
- **Well Delivery**: Drilling Engineering, Completions Engineering
- **Production**: Production Engineering, Facilities Engineering, Operations
- **Asset Management**: Asset Manager (P&L owner), Reserves Coordinator
- **Enabling**: HSE, Land, Regulatory, Finance, Supply Chain

### 4. Authority Structure
- How much autonomy does the Asset Manager have?
- Where does subsurface sit? (central pool vs embedded in asset)
- Who owns the P&L? (asset vs function)
- Capital allocation — centralised or delegated to basin?

## Methodology

1. **Identify peer set** — IOCs, NOCs, independents relevant to the question
2. **Fetch annual report governance sections** — official org chart for each
3. **Extract from corporate websites** — business divisions, leadership pages
4. **Cross-reference with org chart databases** — fill in missing layers
5. **Map each peer across the 4 dimensions** (ownership, pattern, disciplines, authority)
6. **Identify PETRONAS positioning** — where does it fit among peers?
7. **Look for trends** — what direction are peers moving? (e.g., BP reverting to upstream/downstream, Shell dissolving Projects & Technology)

## Key Industry Insights (2026)

- **IOCs are simplifying**: BP reversing Looney's 2020 renewables reorg, going back to clear upstream/downstream. Shell dissolving Projects & Technology, absorbing into business segments.
- **Asset Team is King**: Decision-making decentralised to basin-level Asset Managers who own breakeven cost per barrel (ModalPoint, McKinsey).
- **The "Invisible C-Suite"**: Real decisions happen at the intersection of Asset Team, Technical Authority, Procurement (TCO), and ESG Officer. Published org charts don't show this.
- **Low Carbon as standalone division**: ExxonMobil created Low Carbon Solutions; Shell has Renewables & Energy Solutions. This is becoming standard.
- **PETRONAS's unique dual role**: Carigali (operator) + PMU (regulator/PSC manager) — no other NOC has this exact split.
- **Digital Twin + IT/OT convergence**: Tech teams now have veto power over procurement — if your solution doesn't feed the Digital Twin, it's dead on arrival.

## PETRONAS Context

PETRONAS's upstream is organised through:
- **PETRONAS Carigali Sdn Bhd** — domestic operating arm, runs 60% of Malaysian assets
- **PMU (Petroleum Management Unit)** — PSC management, regulatory oversight of IOCs
- **International Upstream** — blocks in Oman, Canada, etc.
- **Upstream Strategy & Business Development**

The PPGUA 3.0 (PETRONAS Procedures and Guidelines for Upstream Activities) is the governing framework enforced by PMU line departments (POM, PRD, PREX).

## Pitfalls
- Published org charts often hide the real power structure (the "Invisible C-Suite")
- Don't confuse functional hierarchy (who reports to whom) with decision-making authority (who actually decides)
- NOC org charts are often outdated or deliberately opaque — cross-reference multiple sources
- Annual report org charts may be 6-12 months stale by publication date
- JVs and partnerships create complex co-governance structures not shown on parent org charts
- Upstream and Downstream have fundamentally different org cultures — don't assume one pattern fits both
