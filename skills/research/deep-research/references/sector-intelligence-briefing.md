# Sector Intelligence Briefing Pattern

Structured pattern for deep research on an **entire industry sector** — covering multiple company categories, structural dynamics, regional variation, and strategic implications. Broader than a single-company deep dive; more structured than a general research synthesis.

## When to Use

- User says "deep research on [industry sector]" (e.g., "deep research on IOC and NOC")
- User wants to understand the **full competitive landscape** of an industry
- Task requires comparing different categories of players (e.g., US vs European, state-owned vs private)
- Cross-geography analysis needed (e.g., Africa vs Middle East vs Asia)
- The sector has a **local dimension** (user's country NOC, local regulatory context)

## Procedure

### Phase 1: Define the Categories

Identify the structural categories that define the sector. For oil & gas: IOC vs NOC. For banking: Islamic vs conventional, domestic vs foreign. For tech: big tech vs startup vs platform.

Build the **comparison table** as the foundation:

| Dimension | Category A | Category B |
|---|---|---|
| Who | Names + market caps | Names + market caps |
| Ownership | Public vs government | Government vs hybrid |
| Mandate | Shareholder value | Dual mandate |
| Resources | Must acquire | Sovereign reserves |
| Capital discipline | Strict hurdle rates | Variable, fiscal-influenced |

Run 3-4 parallel search batches, each targeting a different category or angle.

### Phase 2: Structural Power Metrics

Gather the **data that proves the power dynamic**:

- % of global reserves controlled by each category
- Total production share
- Debt capacity comparison (NOCs often have 2x the debt capacity of IOCs)
- Exploration spend trends (rising/falling per category)
- M&A volume by category over time

**Critical:** Present as proportions and relative scale, not just absolute numbers. The relationship between categories is the insight.

### Phase 3: Convergence & Divergence

Identify **two opposing forces**:

**Convergence** — how categories are becoming more similar:
- NOCs adopting IOC behaviours (IPOs, international M&A, downstream integration, public listings)
- IOCs forming deeper partnerships with NOCs (JVs, technology-sharing, co-investment)
- "National champions going global" narrative

**Divergence** — where categories are pulling apart:
- US vs European supermajors on energy transition
- Middle Eastern NOCs expanding internationally vs Asian NOCs retrenching
- Regulatory-driven divergence (federal vs state control, PSC vs concession regimes)

### Phase 4: Regional Deep Dives

Pick 1-2 **key regions** where the sector dynamics are playing out most visibly. For each:

| Element | What to cover |
|---|---|
| **Investment flows** | IOC capital committed, NOC expansion plans, total $ figures |
| **Players** | Which IOCs are active, which NOCs dominate |
| **NOC strategy** | State-led expansion, partnerships, production targets |
| **IOC strategy** | Deep-water, LNG megaprojects, divestments |
| **Risk factors** | Security, regulatory instability, financing delays |
| **Regulatory catalyst** | What policy change unlocked/released investment |

### Phase 5: Local Deep Dive (the User's Context)

If the user is in/from a country with a significant sector presence, add a dedicated case study:

1. **Domestic NOC profile** — history, reserves, production trajectory (declining/stable/growing)
2. **Recent strategic moves** — international deals, partnerships, supply agreements, transition strategy
3. **Domestic headwinds** — reserve decline, regulatory disputes, federal-state resource conflicts, revenue pressure
4. **Legal/regulatory timeline** — if there's an active dispute, build a chronological timeline with court decisions, amounts, and current status
5. **IOC relationship** — which IOCs operate in country, nature of relationship (PSC partner, dispute, JV)
6. **Contagion risk** — could this dispute pattern spread to other regions/states?

### Phase 6: Strategic Implications

Synthesize 5-6 strategic takeaways:

1. **Who's winning** — the category-level trend
2. **Existential question** — what each category faces
3. **Growth vector** — what every player is chasing (e.g., gas/LNG as the transition fuel)
4. **Local risk/opportunity** — what the user's ecosystem should watch
5. **Differentiator** — what separates winners from losers
6. **Hidden variable** — underpriced risk that sell-side models miss

## Output Structure

```
## 1. DEFINITIONS — The Two Tribes
  → Comparison table

## 2. THE STRUCTURAL POWER SHIFT
  → Key data points, trend lines

## 3. THE CONVERGENCE TREND
  → NOCs acting like IOCs, partnership patterns

## 4. SECTOR STRATEGIC DIVERGENCE
  → Within-category splits

## 5. REGIONAL DEEP DIVE: [Region]
  → Investment flows, players, risk factors

## 6. LOCAL CASE STUDY: [User's Country/Context]
  → NOC profile, disputes, strategy, legal timeline

## 7. STRATEGIC IMPLICATIONS
  → 5-6 synthesised takeaways
```

## Verification

Before delivering:
- [ ] Structural categories defined with clear comparison?
- [ ] Power metrics backed by specific source data?
- [ ] Convergence AND divergence both covered?
- [ ] Regional dimension present?
- [ ] User's local context addressed?
- [ ] Strategic implications synthesised, not just summarised?

## Pitfalls

- **Don't just describe.** Every section serves a claim about power, trajectory, or strategic choice.
- **Don't skip the local case study.** The global picture frames the user's local problem.
- **Don't miss the divergence dimension.** One-sided convergence is incomplete.
- **Legal disputes need timelines.** Chronological tables > narrative alone.
- **Regulatory stability > geology.** Policy environment often matters more than resource quality.
