# Zinc L-Carnosine (Polaprezinc) — Clinical Evidence Bank

## Overview

**Active ingredient:** Zinc L-Carnosine (Polaprezinc)  
**Japanese patent name:** PepZinGI™ (branded version)  
**Clinical use:** Gastric ulcer treatment, gut lining repair  
**History:** 30+ years in clinical use in Japan  
**Mechanism:** Adheres to gastric mucosa, releases zinc and L-carnosine locally for tissue repair and antioxidant protection

## Key Clinical Studies

### 1. Peptic Ulcer Healing (8-week RCT, n=258)
- **Design:** Randomized controlled trial comparing Zinc L-Carnosine (150mg/day) vs cetraxate hydrochloride (800mg/day)
- **Results:**
  - 60.4% patients achieved endoscopic recovery (Stage S1) after 8 weeks (p<0.05)
  - 78.4% patients achieved endoscopic recovery (Stage H2 and above) after 8 weeks (p<0.05)
  - 81% symptom reduction at 4 weeks
  - 92% symptom reduction at 8 weeks
- **Symptoms improved:** Gastric pain, bloating, flatulence, heartburn, nausea

### 2. H. pylori Eradication (Adjunct Therapy)
- **Finding:** 20% higher H. pylori eradication rate when combined with Triple Therapy
- **Mechanism:** Polaprezinc improves mucosal protection and may enhance antibiotic efficacy

### 3. NSAID-Induced Gastritis Prevention
- **Evidence:** Potentially effective in preventing NSAID-induced stomach ulcer
- **Use case:** Patients on long-term NSAIDs (pain management, arthritis)

### 4. Post-ESD Ulcer Prevention
- **Evidence:** Potentially effective when combined with Proton Pump Inhibitor (PPI)
- **Use case:** After endoscopic submucosal dissection procedures

## Dosage

**Standard clinical dose:** 75mg twice daily (150mg/day total)
- Provides approximately 32-34mg elemental zinc daily
- Typical composition: 22% zinc, 78% L-carnosine

**Duration:** 8 weeks for significant healing; maintenance may continue longer

## Mechanism of Action

1. **Mucosal adhesion:** Preferentially binds to ulcerated/damaged areas
2. **Gradual release:** Releases zinc and L-carnosine locally
3. **Zinc effects:** Contributes to protein synthesis and cell proliferation for tissue repair
4. **L-carnosine effects:** Provides antioxidant protection against reactive oxygen species
5. **Anti-inflammatory:** Stimulates gut cells to produce anti-inflammatory effects
6. **Cytoprotection:** Promotes cell protection in the gut
7. **Heat shock protein:** Increases heat shock protein expression for mucosal protection

## Safety Profile

- **Long-term safety:** Well-established safety profile based on long-term use in humans
- **Adverse events:** No adverse events reported in clinical studies
- **Zinc levels:** Serum zinc levels may increase (monitor if concerned about copper/zinc balance)
- **Copper interaction:** May decrease serum copper levels — monitor if on long-term high-dose therapy

## Delivery Methods

| Method | Pros | Cons |
|--------|------|------|
| **Chewable tablet** | Easy compliance, faster onset | May have shorter contact time with stomach lining |
| **Standard tablet** | Longer contact time | Slower dissolution |
| **Capsule** | Protected from stomach acid | May not adhere as well to gastric mucosa |
| **Slow-release** | Prolonged contact | May not be available in all formulations |

**Clinical preference:** Standard tablets or slow-release formulations may provide better mucosal adhesion than chewable tablets, but compliance is key — if chewable ensures the patient takes it consistently, that is more important than theoretical delivery advantages.

## Regulatory Status

**Approved in:**
- Japan (original approval for gastric ulcer treatment)
- USA (as dietary supplement, not drug)
- Canada, New Zealand, Australia, Korea

**Malaysia:** Available as food supplement (not registered as drug)

## Contraindications / Precautions

- **Pregnancy/breastfeeding:** Insufficient data — avoid unless prescribed
- **Copper deficiency:** May worsen copper deficiency due to zinc competition
- **Antibiotic interaction:** May interfere with tetracycline and quinolone antibiotics — separate by 2+ hours
- **Penicillamine:** Zinc may reduce efficacy — avoid concurrent use

## When to Recommend

**Good candidate for Zinc L-Carnosine:**
- Chronic gastritis or gastric ulcer
- H. pylori infection (as adjunct to triple therapy)
- NSAID-induced gastric irritation
- Recurrent bloating, heartburn, gastric pain
- Post-ESD or other gastric procedures
- Want to reduce PPI dependency

**Not a good candidate:**
- Acute severe symptoms (needs immediate medical attention)
- Suspected perforation or bleeding (emergency)
- Allergy to zinc or carnosine
- Already on high-dose zinc supplementation

## Bottom Line

Zinc L-Carnosine is a **legitimate, clinically-proven ingredient** for gastric health with 30+ years of use. However, branded products (ZettaCare, PepZinGI) are **overpriced versions** of the same ingredient available as generic Polaprezinc. The clinical evidence supports efficacy for gastric ulcer healing, symptom reduction, and H. pylori eradication adjunct therapy.

**Recommendation:** If the patient can afford it and prefers branded/convenient formulations, ZettaCare is fine. If they want value, generic Polaprezinc (RM50-80) provides the same clinical benefit at half the price.
